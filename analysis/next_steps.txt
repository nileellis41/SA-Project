SLEEVE 1 - NEXT STEPS
=====================
Source: analysis/trade_guide.md and analysis/portfolio_diagnostics/report.md.
Rules for every item below:
  - Write the rule and its fitted parameters down BEFORE running it.
  - Run it walk-forward through analysis/experiments/pipeline.py.
  - Report net Sharpe at the flat AND the time-varying cost.
  - Add it to the trial count (31 documented so far) and re-run deflated Sharpe.
  - Do not change run_sleeve1.HEADLINE or run.BOOK after seeing results.


1. CUT COST (ranked)
--------------------
[ ] 1a. Freeze position size at entry   (do first: mechanical, almost no free parameters)
      Why: resizing + sleeve leverage changes are 14-17% of cost. The 25% no-trade
           band (resize_25pct) already added +0.06 to +0.09 Sharpe.
      How: hold fixed DV01 from entry to exit; let the drawdown overlay handle risk.

[ ] 1b. Cost-aware entry   (untested; biggest lever)
      Rule: enter only if (|z| - z_exit) * sigma_eq_bp > k * 2 * unit_cost_bp,
            with k >= 1 fitted per fold on the training window.
      Why: drops the low-vol trades, which lose 1.25 sigma each (headline).
           In-sample upper bound (look-ahead, not a result): PCA-3 sparse goes
           from about 0 to +0.26 sigma per trade.
      Inputs: sigma_eq is now logged per bar by the [diag-logging] change in pca.py.
      Catch: at k = 1 the rule barely binds. 92% of PCA-2 entries already pass
             (median expected reversion 1.67 bp vs 0.73 bp round trip), because the
             60-bar OU overstates the move (mean realised gross is about 0.4 bp a trade).
             Fit k per fold (expect k well above 1) or use the vol-floor variant.
      Simpler variant: no new entries when trailing residual vol < X bp/day, X per fold.

[ ] 1c. Fix the PCA-3 sparse 2010-13 defect
      Why: a nearly singular 2y/5y/10y hedge gave the 3y member weights of
           +68 / -121 / +69. Trades entered in those folds lost 67 risk units
           (whole model: +9).
      How: cap sum|w| or the condition number well below 1e8, or skip the member
           until the 7y has a full training window. Counts as a trial.

[ ] 1d. Slow the signal   (untested hypothesis)
      Why: the 60-bar OU half-life is 6.5 bars (median); the 5-year training fit
           says 103 (PCA-2) / 32 (PCA-3 sparse). Trades are shorter and more
           frequent than the training fit suggests.
      How: OU window 120 or 250 bars, or a minimum holding period.
      Risk: gross may fall with turnover (as with Leung-Li).

[ ] 1e. Futures execution as a separate cost scenario
      Rough estimate: about 0.1 bp per leg (half tick / contract DV01, TU/FV/TY/US).
      This is MY estimate, not from the data. Confirm ticks, commissions and
      micro-contract costs with AMP first. Do not use the Bloomberg TY1 bid/ask
      (about 2,500 missing days). Never make this the baseline.

Skip (already measured): leg netting (saves 0.5-5.5%), charging refit
re-hedges (<= 0.035% pa), entry band 2.5/3.0 alone (too few trades),
Leung-Li levels (turnover falls 5x but gross Sharpe goes to -0.05),
20-bar time stop (hurts PCA-3 sparse by -0.26).

--------------------------
Leakage rule: lag every series to its PUBLIC RELEASE date, not its as-of date.
Cache raw downloads under data/raw/<source>/ (gitignored).

------------------------  -----------------------------  ------------------------------------------------

                          (already in data/raw/treasury) lean into post-auction richening.
                                                         Note: the 5y auction-week test was not significant

gates). Test one at a time and count each as a trial.


3. TENOR NOTES (for interpreting results and choosing what to test)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Front end (2y, 3y)
  Anchored to Fed policy expectations. Residuals come from shifts in the
  expected rate path, auction cycles, and money-fund and bank flows.
  - 2y: cheapest to trade, highest net win rate. Least bad.
  - 3y: strongest gross edge, but most expensive. Wedged between 2y and 5y,
        so hedging it needs larger offsetting weights (see 1c). Data from 2005,
        but it trades only from 2010 (it needs a full 5-year training window).

Belly (5y, 7y)
  Driven by mortgage convexity hedging and the Fed path 2-5 years out.
  - Weakest results. 5y has almost no gross edge. Belly dislocations often
    persist (curvature trends with the Fed cycle), so fading them loses.
  - 7y: only 14 trades; data from 2009-02-26, trades only from 2014. Too
    small a sample to conclude much.
  Candidate test: MBS duration (section 2) as a gate on 5y/7y members only.

Long end (10y, 30y)
  Driven by term premium, supply, inflation expectations, and pension/insurer demand.
  - 10y: solid gross edge, high cost. Best candidate for 1b (cost-aware entry).
  - 30y: moderate edge, moderate cost. Noisier residual. Under the
    time-varying table its cost is 1.5x the other tenors.
  Candidate test: refunding sizes (section 2) as a supply flag on 10y/30y.

Note: a tenor enters the PCA only once it has a full 5-year training window.
The PCA uses 2y/5y/10y/30y until 2010-05, adds the 3y then, and has all six
tenors only from 2014-04, when the 7y enters. Results for every tenor before
2014 come from the 4- and 5-tenor setups (report.md, Step 6).
